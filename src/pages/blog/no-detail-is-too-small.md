Think of the micro-interactions you use every day: the bounce at the end of a scroll, the haptic feedback of a tap, or the subtle reflow of the text when you rotate a screen. Tiny details that most people don’t consciously notice, yet they shape our entire perception of polish and craft in a product.

At Resend, we’re always talking browsers. Which one uses the best font? Which one feels smoother? Which one better suits your needs? We’re in a constant pursuit of the perfect experience. Obsessing over these small details isn’t indulgence. As Massimo Vignelli wrote in The Vignelli Canon, “Every detail is important because the end result is the sum of all the details involved in the creative process.”

If the end result is the sum of all small details, then taste is what ties them together. Taste isn’t just preference. Taste is discernment. The ability to navigate infinite possibilities and decide what belongs, what doesn’t, and in what proportion. It is something you build with time: by savoring details, by noticing what moves you and why, by trusting your instincts, and by grounding yourself in craft.

In a world of digital noise, where technology races to the bottom of efficiency, obsessing over small details becomes an act of nonconformity. To insist on taste, on intention, on details no one sees but everyone feels, is to resist forgetting that design is ultimately human.
